Privacy Policy — The Last Bell

Effective date: 18 October 2026 · Updated 19 October 2026 to cover optional purchases and Google Play Games, 24 October 2026 for cloud saves, 31 October 2026 to add play statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase), and 4 November 2026 to add optional videos (Google AdMob) (the Peal: 18 October 2026)

This policy explains what information the mobile game The Last Bell (“the game”) handles. The game is published by BlairsLab (“we”, “us”). If you have a question, contact us at privacy@blairslab.co.nz.

Summary

  • The game has no accounts and no chat. We do not ask for your name, email, location, contacts or photos.
  • Your progress is saved only on your device.
  • The Peal, a fortnightly competition, is optional. Only if you join it, the game sends our server a random player id that the game made up, your display name in the Peal, and your best waves in it, so your group’s board can be shown.
  • There is no chat and no way for players to contact each other: other players in your Peal group see only display names and best waves.
  • The game sends anonymous play statistics (how long it is played and how far players get) to Google Analytics for Firebase. They never include your name, and you can switch them off in Settings.
  • Videos are always optional: one only plays if you choose to watch it for a small reward (never in the Peal). When you do, Google AdMob receives device and ad information to show it. Optional in-app purchases are made through Google Play (see below).
  • We do not share or sell personal information.

1. Information stored on your device

So you can continue playing, the game saves on your device: your progress (runs, towers, Bell-metal, Foundry upgrades and best wave), your Peal world if you join one, which first-time hints you have seen, and your settings. This stays in the game’s private storage on your device.

2. The Peal (optional)

The Peal is an optional fortnightly competition. If you choose to join it, the game sends our server, which runs on Amazon Web Services in Sydney, Australia:

  • a random player id created by the game when it was first opened. It is not linked to your device’s identifiers, your Google account or anything else about you;
  • your display name in the Peal. this is a name the game makes up for you (for example “Keeper Wren of Hollowmere”), or, if you have signed in to Google Play Games, your Play Games name;
  • for each Peal you take part in: which group you are in, your best wave and how long that run had lasted (used to check scores are possible).

Other players in your group of up to 100 see your display name and best wave. Nobody can send you messages, and we do not use this information for anything except running the Peal. It is sent encrypted (HTTPS) and stored in a database only we can access. If you never join a Peal, nothing is sent.

3. Information we collect otherwise

Apart from the optional Peal and the play statistics below, we do not collect information through the game. The game does not request location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or similar permissions, and it contains no crash-reporting services of our own.

Play statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase). To understand how the game is played and where it can be improved, the game uses Google Analytics for Firebase, a service of Google LLC. It collects:

  • a random app-instance identifier created for this game on your device (not your advertising ID, which the game does not use for statistics);
  • device information, such as device model, Android version and language, and the country derived from your IP address (the IP address itself is not stored);
  • how the game is used: when it is opened, how long it is played, on how many different days, and game milestones (finishing the opening and the guided first run, waves reached, recasts, the Open Field, the Peal and Trials), plus purchase events reported by Google Play. If you uninstall the game, Android tells Analytics, together with how far you had got.

This information is not linked to your name, your Peal player id or your Google account, is not used for advertising or ad personalisation, and is not sold. Google processes it for us as described in Privacy and Security in Firebase and the Google Privacy Policy. It is kept for up to 14 months and then deleted automatically. You can switch play statistics off at any time in the game: open Settings and set Play statistics to Off. Nothing more is collected after that.

Google Play may send us anonymous crash and performance reports through its Android vitals service, depending on your device settings. These do not identify you.

3a. Optional videos (Google AdMob)

In Trials and between runs (never in the Peal), the game can offer an optional video in return for a small reward, such as an extra rewind, a second chance, double Bell-metal or faster research. A video only plays when you choose to watch one; there are no forced ads, and at most ten can be offered a day. Ads are provided by Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC, and are limited to content suitable for general audiences. To show and measure ads and prevent fraud, AdMob may collect:

  • device information, such as device model, operating system, language and screen size;
  • the device’s advertising identifier, where permitted;
  • your IP address and the approximate location derived from it;
  • information about the ads shown and your interactions with them, and diagnostic information about ad performance.

This information is encrypted in transit. If you are in a region that requires it, such as the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, Google’s consent form asks whether ads may be personalised before any ad loads. How Google uses this information is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy. You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your Android device settings under Google > Ads (or Privacy > Ads).

4. Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

5. Keeping and deleting your information

Game data on your device stays there until you delete it. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes your progress; we cannot recover it.

Peal records (player id, display name, groups and best waves) are kept so boards and placings can be shown, and for up to 12 months after a Peal ends. To have your Peal records deleted sooner, email privacy@blairslab.co.nz with your display name and the Peal number shown in the game, and we will delete them within 30 days.

6. Your rights

Depending on where you live, including under Australia’s Privacy Act 1988, New Zealand’s Privacy Act 2020, the EU and UK GDPR, and US state privacy laws, you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal information about you. Contact us at privacy@blairslab.co.nz.

7. Changes to this policy

Purchases

Optional purchases are handled entirely by Google Play. We never see your payment details. The game checks Google’s signed receipt on your device and keeps a scrambled (hashed) record of each purchase in its save on your phone, so a purchase is never granted twice and can be restored. No purchase information is sent to our server.

Google Play Games

Signing in to Google Play Games is optional. If you do, your best wave, Wardens mended and Tolls stopped are sent to Google Play Games leaderboards, which Google runs under its own privacy policy, and your Play Games name is used as your display name in the Peal (see above). While you are signed in, the game also keeps a copy of your save (your progress, as stored on your device) in your own Google Play Games account, so you can carry on from another phone; that copy is held by Google under its own privacy policy, and you can delete it from your Google Play Games settings. We do not receive your Google account details.

If a later version of the game adds other optional features that handle information, we will update this page and its date before that version is released, and note the change in the game’s update notes.
